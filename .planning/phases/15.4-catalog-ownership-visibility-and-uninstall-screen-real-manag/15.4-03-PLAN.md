<objective>
Finish the catalog half of D-02/D-02a: make the Owner column and the focused-row detail bar

Purpose: plan 15.4-01's tracer proved one owner value end-to-end. D-02's governing

</objective>

<design_note>
**The cask register split (Round-1 review HIGH, `15.4-REVIEWS.md` @59a2d69).** The first draft
of this plan required three things that cannot all hold: a distinct cask label in the Owner
column (15.4-01), byte-identical existing cask prose in the detail bar, and no two owners
sharing a detail sentence. `installer/catalog_tui.py:501-502` maps both `"brew"` and `"cask"`
to the identical string `"managed by Homebrew"`, so requirement 2 and requirement 3 are
directly contradictory.

The chosen resolution is the reviewer's first option — **separate the column register from
the detail-bar prose register** — with two amendments that keep the anti-duplication property
the original requirement was really protecting:

1. Both registers live in `installer/ownership.py`, declared adjacently. `catalog_tui.py`
   keeps NO owner-label dict of its own. The failure mode the shared-table requirement
   existed to prevent — two hand-maintained copies drifting — is prevented by locality and by
   two exhaustiveness tests, not by forcing one table to serve two incompatible jobs.
2. Sharing in the prose register is legal but must be DECLARED: `DETAIL_PHRASE_COLLISIONS`
   names `{brew, cask}` as the one intended sharing, and a test fails on any collision not in
   that constant. The column register permits no sharing at all.

Rejected: changing the cask detail wording (it would rewrite user-visible copy `tests/` asserts
verbatim, in a phase whose scope is attribution, not copy); and dropping the no-duplicate rule
outright (it would let a future owner value silently borrow another's sentence).
</design_note>
